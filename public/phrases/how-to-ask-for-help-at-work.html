How to ask for help at work

Asking for help early is a professional skill, not a weakness. These lines help you explain what you need, show what you already tried and respect the other person's time.

Three things that help most
  • Say what you already tried, so the other person starts from there.
  • Make the request specific: 15 minutes, a review, one decision.
  • Thank them and tell them later how it turned out.

40 phrases you can use

  • I'm stuck on this, and I'd really appreciate a second pair of eyes.
  • Do you have ten minutes this afternoon? I'd like your advice on something.
  • I've tried a few things already. Here's what didn't work.
  • I don't want to waste your time, so I'll keep this short.
  • Could you show me how you'd approach this?
  • I'm not sure I understood the task. Could you explain it once more?
  • I'd rather ask now than get it wrong later.
  • Would you be willing to review my draft before I send it?
  • I'm falling behind on this, and I need to flag it early.
  • Can we split this task? I can take the first half.
  • Who would be the best person to ask about this?
  • I'm new here, and I'm still learning how things work.
  • Could you point me to an example I could learn from?
  • I need help prioritizing. Which of these should come first?
  • Thank you for helping me. I'll pay it forward.
  • I'd like some guidance, not for you to do it for me.
  • Could we pair on this for half an hour?
  • I'm overwhelmed this week. Can we move one deadline?
  • I made a mistake, and I need help fixing it quickly.
  • What would you do in my position?

How to talk to your manager about work, workload and growth

Your relationship with your manager shapes your day-to-day work. These lines help you share updates, push back on priorities, ask for support and talk about your career.

Three things that help most
  • Bring a problem together with two possible options.
  • Ask your manager to rank priorities when everything is urgent.
  • Talk about growth regularly, not only at review time.

40 phrases you can use

  • Do you have fifteen minutes this week for a quick catch-up?
  • I'd like to get your view on my priorities for the next month.
  • Here's a short update on what I finished this week.
  • I'm stuck on one thing and would value your advice.
  • I have three tasks marked urgent. Which one should come first?
  • I'd like to take on more responsibility in this area.
  • Can I tell you about something that's been bothering me at work?
  • I want to make sure we're aligned on what success looks like here.
  • What would you like me to do more of, and what less of?
  • I've noticed my workload has grown a lot recently.
  • At this pace, something will slip. Can we look at it together?
  • I'd like your support on this, because I can't solve it alone.
  • I don't agree with this decision, and I'd like to explain why.
  • I'll go with your decision, but I wanted you to hear my concerns.
  • Can you help me understand the reasoning behind this change?
  • I'd like to work more independently on the next project.
  • Could we move our one-to-one to a time that works better for both of us?
  • Here's a problem, and here are two options I see.
  • I'd recommend the second option, and here's why.
  • I want to be upfront: I made a mistake on the report.

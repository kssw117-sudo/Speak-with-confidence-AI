How to tell your boss or client a project is late

Bad news gets worse the longer you wait. These lines help you flag a delay early, explain what happened, propose a plan and protect trust.

Three things that help most
  • Flag the risk as soon as you see it, not on the due date.
  • Come with a new realistic date and what you can deliver now.
  • Send short regular updates until it is back on track.

40 phrases you can use

  • I want to flag this early: the deadline is at risk.
  • I'll be honest with you, we won't make Friday as planned.
  • Here is where we are, what is blocking us, and what I suggest.
  • I can deliver the core part on time and the rest by Tuesday.
  • What matters most to you if we can't have everything by the deadline?
  • I underestimated how long the testing would take, and that's on me.
  • I'd rather tell you now than surprise you on the due date.
  • To hit the original date, we would need to drop one feature.
  • Can we agree on a new date that I'm confident I can keep?
  • I have a plan to catch up, and I'd like to walk you through it.
  • The delay comes from a dependency we don't control.
  • We're waiting on approval from legal, and that's holding us up.
  • I've already reached out to them and expect an answer by tomorrow.
  • I'll send you a short update every day until this is back on track.
  • Realistically, I need two more days to do this properly.
  • I can rush it, but the quality will suffer. Which do you prefer?
  • Let's look at what can move and what can't.
  • If I take this on, the other project will slip by a week.
  • Could you help me decide which of these two deadlines comes first?
  • I'm on track for the deadline, and I'll let you know if that changes.

How to give feedback without sounding harsh

Useful feedback is specific, kind and about behavior, not personality. These lines help you start the conversation, describe what happened, explain the impact and agree on what changes next.

Three things that help most
  • Describe one specific situation, not "you always".
  • Explain the impact on the work, the team or the client.
  • End with a clear request and ask how you can help.

40 phrases you can use

  • Can I share something I noticed? It's meant to help, not criticize.
  • I want to give you some feedback on the presentation. Is now a good time?
  • Overall the report was strong. There's one part I think could be clearer.
  • I noticed the deadline slipped twice this month. Can we talk about what's happening?
  • I'm bringing this up because I think you can do even better.
  • Here's what worked really well, and here's one thing to change next time.
  • When the meeting ran over, it meant the team missed lunch. Let's keep it to time.
  • I'd like to understand your side before I say anything else.
  • This isn't about you as a person. It's about this specific piece of work.
  • What do you think went well, and what would you do differently?
  • I've seen real improvement in how you handle client calls.
  • I need you to send updates before the end of the day, not after.
  • Can we agree on a plan for the next two weeks?
  • How can I support you in getting this right?
  • I want to be direct, because I think you'd prefer that.
  • I'm concerned about the tone in some of your emails to the team.
  • The quality is there. The timing is what needs work.
  • Let's pick one thing to focus on, rather than everything at once.
  • I should have mentioned this earlier, and that's on me.
  • I really appreciated how you handled that difficult customer.

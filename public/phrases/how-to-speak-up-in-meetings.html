How to speak up in meetings

Many people have good ideas but never find the moment to say them. These lines help you join the discussion, disagree politely, handle interruptions and keep the meeting on track.

Three things that help most
  • Speak in the first ten minutes. It gets harder the longer you wait.
  • Build on what someone said before adding your point.
  • If you are interrupted, calmly say you would like to finish your thought.

40 phrases you can use

  • Can I add something here?
  • I'd like to build on what Maria just said.
  • Before we move on, I have a concern I'd like to raise.
  • I see it a little differently, and here's why.
  • Can we take a step back and look at the goal?
  • Sorry to interrupt, but I think we're talking about two different things.
  • Let me summarize what I'm hearing, to make sure we're aligned.
  • I'd like to finish my point, and then I'm happy to hear yours.
  • What's the decision we need to make today?
  • Who's going to own this action item?
  • Can we park that topic and come back to it at the end?
  • I have a quick question about the timeline.
  • I don't think we have enough data to decide yet.
  • Could we hear from people who haven't spoken yet?
  • I agree with the direction, but I'm worried about the budget.
  • Let me play devil's advocate for a moment.
  • That's a good idea. How would it work in practice?
  • I want to make sure we're not missing any risks.
  • We're running out of time. Let's agree on next steps.
  • I'll send a summary after the meeting.

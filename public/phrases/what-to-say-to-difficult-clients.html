What to say to a difficult client

Angry, demanding or vague clients test your patience. These phrases help you stay professional, acknowledge the problem, set limits and move the conversation toward a solution.

Three things that help most
  • Acknowledge the feeling before you explain anything.
  • Repeat back what the client wants in one sentence to check you understood.
  • Offer two options instead of a yes or no, so the client still feels in control.

40 phrases you can use

  • I hear you, and I want to get this right. Walk me through exactly where it broke down.
  • That's fair feedback. Here's what I can fix, and here's what's outside what we agreed to.
  • I understand the frustration. Let's separate what's urgent from what's important here.
  • I don't think leaving solves the actual problem. Can we try one more fix first?
  • I'd rather tell you the truth now than promise something I can't deliver.
  • Let's agree on what fixed actually looks like before I start.
  • I want this relationship to work, so tell me what would need to change.
  • Thank you for telling me directly. That helps me fix it faster.
  • You're right, that shouldn't have happened, and I'm sorry.
  • Let me make sure I understand. The main issue is the delay, not the quality?
  • Here's what I'm going to do today, and here's when you'll hear from me.
  • I can see why that looks wrong from your side. Let me explain what happened.
  • I'm not going to argue about who's at fault. Let's focus on solving it.
  • That request is outside the original scope. I'm happy to quote it separately.
  • I can do it faster, but it would cost more. Which matters more to you right now?
  • I want to be honest with you about what's realistic by Friday.
  • Can we look at the agreement together, so we're on the same page?
  • I understand this is stressful. I'm on your side here.
  • Let's set a short call each week, so nothing surprises either of us.
  • I'm happy to make changes. Can you give me all of them in one list?
